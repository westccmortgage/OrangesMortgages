How to get pre-approved for a mortgage

In California's fast, competitive market, a pre-approval is your ticket to being taken seriously. Here's exactly what it is, the documents you'll need, and how to get one — often within 24–48 hours.

Pre-qualification vs pre-approval

People use these terms interchangeably, but they're different:

  • Pre-qualification is a quick, informal estimate based on numbers you share. Useful for a ballpark — not much weight with sellers.
  • Pre-approval is the real thing: the lender verifies your income, assets, and credit and issues a letter stating what you can borrow. This is what makes your offer credible.

Bottom line: If you're seriously shopping in California, get pre-approved, not just pre-qualified.

Why it matters in California

California markets move fast and often have multiple offers. A solid pre-approval:

  • Tells you your true budget before you fall in love with a home.
  • Signals to sellers and agents that you're a serious, ready buyer.
  • Speeds up your closing once you're in contract.

Documents you'll need

Having these ready is what makes pre-approval fast:

  • Income: recent pay stubs, W-2s, and often 1–2 years of tax returns.
  • Assets: recent bank and investment/asset statements (for your down payment and reserves).
  • Identity: a government photo ID.
  • Self-employed? Expect additional business documentation — see our note on bank-statement options when we build your plan.

The steps & timeline

  1. Apply & share documents. A short application plus the paperwork above.
  2. Credit & verification. The lender reviews credit, income, assets, and your debt-to-income.
  3. Pre-approval letter issued. Often within 24–48 hours — showing your price range.
  4. Shop with confidence. Make offers backed by a real letter.

Tips to strengthen your file

  • Avoid opening new credit or making big purchases before closing.
  • Keep your down-payment funds in place and documented — see down payment basics.
  • Respond quickly to document requests; speed keeps your file moving.

Questions

Pre-approval FAQ

What's the difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval?

Pre-qualification is a quick, informal estimate. Pre-approval is stronger — the lender verifies your income, assets, and credit and issues a letter showing what you can borrow. Sellers take a pre-approval far more seriously.

How long does mortgage pre-approval take?

For many buyers, within 24–48 hours once the lender has your basic documents. Gathering paperwork ahead of time is what makes it fast.

What documents do I need?

Typically recent pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns, bank/asset statements, and a photo ID. Self-employed borrowers may provide extra business documentation. Your lender will give you an exact checklist.

Does getting pre-approved hurt my credit?

A mortgage credit check is a hard inquiry with a small, temporary effect. Rate-shopping several lenders within a short window is generally treated as a single inquiry, so comparing offers usually doesn't stack up separate hits.

How long does a pre-approval last?

Commonly around 60–90 days, since your credit and income info needs to stay current. If it expires while you're still shopping, it can usually be refreshed quickly.
